Label Learning Guide

Open any section for a quick explanation of what the numbers on a Nutrition Facts label mean.

1Serving information
Serving size
The amount used to calculate every number on the label. Compare it with how much you actually plan to eat or drink.
Servings per container
How many servings are in the entire package. If you eat two servings, most listed amounts are doubled.
2Calories & Daily Value
Calories
A measure of energy in one serving. Individual energy needs vary, so calories are most useful in context with serving size and the rest of the label.
% Daily Value (%DV)
A quick way to see whether one serving is relatively low or high in a nutrient. As a general guide, 5% DV or less is low and 20% DV or more is high.
3Fats & cholesterol
Total fat
The combined amount of fat in one serving, including saturated, unsaturated, and trans fats.
Saturated fat
One type of dietary fat. The %DV can help you compare how much different foods contain.
Trans fat
Listed in grams. Nutrition Facts labels do not show a % Daily Value for trans fat.
Cholesterol
A waxy substance found in animal-derived foods. The label shows both the amount in milligrams and its %DV.
4Carbohydrates & sugars
Total carbohydrate
Includes starches, fiber, and sugars in one serving.
Dietary fiber
A type of carbohydrate the body does not fully digest. Fiber supports digestive health and can help with fullness.
Total sugars
All sugars in the serving, including naturally occurring sugars and added sugars.
Added sugars
Sugars added during processing or preparation. This amount is included within Total Sugars, not added on top of it.
5Sodium & protein
Sodium
An essential mineral and a major component of salt. The label helps you compare sodium amounts among foods and serving sizes.
Protein
A nutrient the body uses to build and repair muscles, tissues, enzymes, and other structures.
Why labels matter

Nutrition labels make it easier to compare foods, notice serving-size differences, and understand what is actually in a portion.
